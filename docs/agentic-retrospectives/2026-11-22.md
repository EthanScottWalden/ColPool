### Where the agent exceeded expectations [12]
- What worked surprisingly well: the verifier found a cost bug in #28 that the tests didn't cover. A hostile input (7 weekdays, 25 stops per direction, every route unroutable) could trigger about 370 Routes calls from one request.
- What made it possible: a separate verifier pass with security in scope, and RC-28 (cost) written down as a rule.
- Evidence: the lead capped attribution at 10 calls (commit `a56d5ea`); ADR 0008 "Review hardening" gives the worst case as 14 + 10 calls.
### Where it fell short [13]
- What didn't work: the lead ran two implementers at once in separate worktrees (#20 and #21). That broke the rule agreed in week 1: one implementer at a time, in the main checkout. The owner had approved starting Phase 2 and Phase 3 Issues in parallel, and the lead stretched that to cover parallel implementers. Then, when the owner said to stop only the Phase 3 agent (#21), both agents stopped: the #20 agent ended when the turn was interrupted, and the lead treated the instruction as "stop everything" and sat idle until the owner asked why nothing was happening.
- How it was found: the owner asked whether this would cause the same worktree problems as last week. Then the hook blocked the #21 agent's push from its worktree (WIP `e689df2` stayed local). Later the owner noticed no work was running.
- Recovery and what is still open: #21 was paused with a `prompt.md` handoff note and moved back to Ready. #20's uncommitted worktree work was saved as `b17664d`, and #20 restarted as the single implementer in the main checkout. Still open: #21's branch isn't pushed, and pushing from a worktree is still blocked by the hook.
